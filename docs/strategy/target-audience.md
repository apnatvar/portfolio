# Target audience

## Primary

Small product companies and founders with a live site or a defined product brief. Typical contacts are a founder, product lead, or engineering lead who needs specialist help without a large team handoff.

## Secondary

Marketing and SEO leads working with a Next.js or other modern web application who need an engineer to diagnose or implement technical recommendations. Also relevant: teams with repeated SEO data or reporting workflows that are good candidates for careful automation.

## Trigger situations

- Search visibility or indexation changed after a launch, migration, or redesign.
- A Next.js site has uncertain rendering, metadata, canonical, sitemap, or structured data behavior.
- A growing page set needs templates, data rules, and indexation safeguards.
- Core Web Vitals or perceived speed needs investigation at route/template level.
- Repetitive checks or reporting consume time and have known rules and exceptions.

## What these buyers need to know

Show the problem each service fits, the evidence and access needed, the concrete deliverables, what remains uncertain, and how the work is handed over. Avoid implying that a tool, score, or technical fix alone guarantees search growth.

## Qualification

Good engagements have a reachable site or repository, a clear owner, representative URLs or workflows, and agreement on what counts as a useful deliverable. Redirect broad requests toward a small diagnostic scope before promising implementation outcomes.
