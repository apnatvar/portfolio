# Content strategy

## Editorial purpose

Help a prospective client understand the technical problem, how it can be investigated, what a scoped engagement can deliver, and what evidence can or cannot establish. Content should be useful on its own and direct readers to a relevant service only when that is a natural next step.

## Core content groups

1. **Technical SEO and Next.js:** rendering, metadata, canonicals, structured data, crawl/indexation, sitemaps, and migrations. Keep Next.js SEO under technical SEO.
2. **Programmatic SEO:** intent-to-page mapping, source data quality, useful differentiation, templates, and indexation governance.
3. **SEO automation:** repeatable data workflows, validation, exception handling, and human review.
4. **Web performance:** field and lab evidence, LCP/INP/CLS causes, and template-level engineering choices.

## Formats and cadence

Prefer durable guides, annotated implementation notes, and case-study-style project writeups when evidence permits. No publishing cadence is established; publish when there is a useful, supportable contribution rather than to meet an arbitrary frequency.

## Evidence and review

Check claims against source code, project records, or a named measurement source. Distinguish a shipped implementation from a measured outcome. Label prototypes and open-source work accurately. Do not publish client identifiers, confidential data, or performance and ranking claims without evidence and permission.

## Distribution and learning

Link guides from relevant service pages and portfolio items. Use Search Console query data and qualified inquiry language to refine topics; no volume or paid keyword data is currently available. Review pages for accuracy when the implementation, platform behavior, or cited evidence changes.
